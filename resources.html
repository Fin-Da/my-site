Recommended Resources

  • Universal Declaration of Human Rights (United Nations)

    The official UN page presenting all thirty articles of the UDHR, the document adopted in 1948.

    Why it's useful: It's the primary source for what human rights are, so everything else on this site traces back to it.

  • Office of the High Commissioner for Human Rights (OHCHR)

    The UN agency responsible for monitoring human rights violations around the world.

    Why it's useful: It shows how the UN actually works on human rights day to day, not just what it says on paper.

  • What We Do: Amnesty International

    An independent human rights organization that investigates abuses and advocates for change.

    Why it's useful: It gives a view from outside the UN, including how civil society groups hold governments accountable.

  • Human Rights Watch

    An international non-governmental organization that publishes research reports and news on human rights issues by country.

    Why it's useful: Its country reports are a good way to see how human rights problems look in specific places.

  • Human Rights: United Nations Global Issues

    The UN's overview page on human rights, with background on the UN's work and links to related topics.

    Why it's useful: It's a good starting point for beginners before moving on to more detailed sources.
